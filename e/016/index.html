Yuval and the Shadow That Grew

Yuval is four years old, and he sleeps in a small room with one round nightlight.

The little light sits low on the floor, near the door, and it glows soft and yellow all night long.

Yuval does not like his room to be all black, and he does not like it too bright either.

He likes it just like this: soft and warm, with his bear Kubi under his arm.

"Good night, Kubi," he says. "Good night, little light."

In the middle of the night Yuval woke up, and his mouth was dry as paper.

He climbed down from his bed to find his cup of water, very quietly.

And then he stopped. On the wall there was a giant.

The giant was tall and dark, with a big round head and two long arms.

Yuval did not move at all. He held his breath and looked.

Yuval took one small step back, towards his bed.

The giant took one step too, at exactly the same time.

Yuval lifted his hand to hold on to the bed, and the giant lifted its hand as well.

"Mum," Yuval called, not loud, because his voice had gone very small.

Mum came in with quiet feet and sat down on the floor beside him.

"There is a giant on my wall," Yuval whispered. "It does everything that I do."

Mum looked at the wall, and she did not look worried at all.

"Wave to him," she said. "Wave with your whole arm, as high as you can."

Yuval waved. The giant waved back, high and slow.

"That is not a giant," said Mum. "That is you. That is your shadow."

"Look at the little light," said Mum. "It sends its light out in straight lines, all over the room."

"The light runs to the wall, and the wall turns soft and yellow."

"But you are standing in the way, and light cannot bend around you."

"So behind you the wall stays dark, in exactly your shape."

A shadow is not a thing, Yuval thought. A shadow is a place where the light could not get in.

"Now walk to the light," said Mum. "Slowly, one step at a time."

Yuval took one step, and two, and three, closer and closer to the little lamp.

The giant grew. It grew up the wall, and up, and up, all the way to the ceiling.

When he stood right next to the lamp, his shadow was the biggest thing in the room.

Step, step, close: the shadow grows tall. Step, step, back: the shadow grows small.

"And now walk back to the wall," said Mum, "all the way to the end."

Yuval walked backwards, step by step, until his shoulders touched the cool wall.

His shadow came with him, and it got smaller, and smaller, and smaller.

At the wall it was just his size: a little Yuval made of dark.

Step, step, close: the shadow grows tall. Step, step, back: the shadow grows small.

Now Yuval was not afraid at all, and he wanted to try everything.

He put his two hands together near the lamp and made a bird with wide wings.

He held up two fingers, and a rabbit with two long ears sat down on the wall.

He lifted Kubi up high, and Kubi had a shadow too, round and soft and enormous.

Mum laughed very quietly, so that nobody else in the house would wake up.

Then Yuval got a little bit tired, the way you do at the end of a good game.

He climbed back into his bed, and Mum put Kubi in beside him.

On the wall, right next to his pillow, his own small shadow lay down as well.

"Good night, shadow," said Yuval, and he closed his eyes.

Step, step, close: the shadow grows tall. Step, step, back: the shadow grows small.

Before we sleep

  1. What would you do if you saw a giant on your wall in the middle of the night?
  2. Where do you think your shadow goes when somebody turns the light off?

Tomorrow you can put a torch, or the light of a phone, low on the floor of a dark room. Stand in front of it and walk slowly towards the light, and watch your shadow climb up the wall. Then walk back until you touch the wall, and watch it shrink down to your own size again.

Good night

The little light is shining, and your shadow is asleep on the wall, right next to you, until the morning.

For grown-ups: what is true here, and sources

A shadow is not a thing but a place. Light travels out from a lamp in straight lines, and it cannot bend around an opaque body, so the wall behind a child stays dark in the child's shape. That is the whole of it, and it is worth saying out loud: nothing is added to the wall, something is missing from it.

The size follows from geometry. A small lamp close by sends its light out in a widening fan, so the nearer the child stands to the lamp, the bigger the slice of that fan the child blocks, and the bigger the shadow on the wall. Walking back towards the wall shrinks the shadow until it is about the child's own size. Roughly: shadow height equals the child's height times the lamp-to-wall distance divided by the lamp-to-child distance.

One honest limit of the story: this trick needs a lamp in the room. Outdoors it does not work, because the Sun is so far away that its rays reach us very nearly parallel, so a sunlit shadow stays about your own size however you walk about; what changes its length there is how high the Sun sits in the sky.
