Mira and the Magnet That Loved Metal

Mira is four years old, and she wants to know how every single thing in the world works.

This evening she stands at Grandpa's kitchen table and swings one foot behind the other.

Grandpa opens his old wooden toolbox, digs around inside it, and puts something small and red in her hand.

It is a magnet, curved like a tiny horseshoe, cool and smooth and heavier than it looks.

"This one loves metal," says Grandpa. "Go on. Find out what it wants to hold."

Mira looks along the table and sees a shiny spoon lying next to the sugar bowl.

She moves the magnet close, and closer, and the spoon jumps up and sticks to it with a little tap.

"It grabbed my spoon!" laughs Mira, and she pulls the spoon off again with two fingers.

Then she tries a nail from the toolbox, and the nail sticks too, straight and proud.

Click! The magnet grabs. Shhh. The magnet lets go.

Next Mira tries the big wooden spoon, the one Grandpa stirs his soup with.

Nothing happens. The wooden spoon does not move even a tiny bit.

She tries a paper napkin, then a plastic cup, then Grandpa's smooth glass jar.

Nothing, nothing, nothing. The magnet lies quiet in her hand, like a small sleeping animal.

"Is my magnet broken?" asks Mira, and her mouth goes small and worried.

"It is not broken," says Grandpa. "Put everything that clicks in one little pile."

So Mira makes two piles on the table: the click pile and the quiet pile.

Into the click pile go the spoon, the nail, a door key, and a small tin lid.

Into the quiet pile go the wooden spoon, the napkin, the cup, and one soft grey sock.

Click! The magnet grabs. Shhh. The magnet lets go.

"Everything in my click pile is metal," says Mira. "So my magnet loves metal."

Grandpa smiles, puts a shiny drink can on the table, and lays a brown coin down beside it.

Mira touches the magnet to the can. Nothing. She touches it to the coin. Nothing again.

"But these are metal too!" she says, and she turns the can around to try the other side.

"Some metals are shy," says Grandpa. "Your magnet only pulls the ones with iron inside them."

"Your spoon has iron in it. The nail has iron. The door key has iron too."

"But this can is made of aluminium, and this coin is copper, and they have no iron at all."

Mira holds the can in one hand and the spoon in the other, and looks at them for a long time.

Click! The magnet grabs. Shhh. The magnet lets go.

Now Mira walks slowly around the whole house with the magnet held out in front of her.

The table leg: quiet. The door handle: click. The window glass: quiet. The radiator: click.

She reaches up as high as she can, and the magnet clicks onto the fridge and holds her drawing there.

"There is iron under that white paint," says Grandpa. "Now everybody can see your picture."

Then Grandpa lays a white sheet of paper flat on the table and puts a paper clip on top of it.

He slides the magnet underneath the paper, and the little clip creeps along all by itself.

Mira laughs so much that she has to hold on to the table.

The pull goes right through the paper, and the magnet never touches the clip at all.

Click! The magnet grabs. Shhh. The magnet lets go.

Later Mira lies in bed, and the magnet rests on the little table beside her.

The spoon sleeps stuck to the magnet, and the nail sleeps stuck to the spoon.

Grandpa turns off the lamp, and the room goes soft and dark and warm.

Mira closes her eyes, slowly, slowly, and hears the quiet words one more time.

Click! The magnet grabs. Shhh. The magnet lets go. Shhh. Good night, Mira.

Before we sleep

  1. If you had Mira's magnet right now, what is the very first thing you would try it on?
  2. Which things in your room do you think would click, and which ones would stay quiet?

Tomorrow you can take a magnet off the fridge and walk around one room together. Touch it to ten things and say it out loud each time: click, or quiet? Keep the two piles on the floor and look at them at the end.

Good night

The magnet holds the spoon, the spoon holds the nail, and your pillow holds you until morning.

For grown-ups: what is true here, and sources

The true part: a magnet pulls strongly only on ferromagnetic materials. In a kitchen that means iron and steel (steel is mostly iron), and also nickel and cobalt. Wood, paper, plastic, glass and cloth are not pulled at all.

Mira's surprise is real, and it is the point of the story: aluminium and copper are metals, yet a fridge magnet does not pull them. Aluminium is very weakly paramagnetic and copper is weakly diamagnetic, thousands of times too weak for a child's hand to feel.

Two honest simplifications. Many kitchen spoons are austenitic stainless steel (18/10), which contains iron but is barely magnetic, so a real spoon may not click; if that happens at your table, try a nail, a paper clip or a key instead. And most fridge doors are painted steel and hold magnets, but some stainless or aluminium fronts do not, so the fridge line is true of most fridges, not all.
