Fen the Firefly's Quiet Lamp

Fen is a little firefly. She is round and soft, with grey wings and two long antennae.

She lives in the tall grass at the edge of a wide green meadow, under one big leaf that is bigger than she is.

At the end of Fen is a tiny lamp. All day long the lamp sleeps. When the sun goes down, it wakes up.

Tonight the sky turns orange, and then purple, and then dark as a soft blue blanket.

Fen opens her wings. Tonight is her very first night to fly out over the meadow.

Fen flies up over the grass, higher and higher and higher. The whole meadow below her is full of little lights!

The other fireflies blink fast. Blink! Blink! Blink! Quick and bright, like tiny falling stars.

Fen wants to say hello to them too, so she tries as hard as she can.

Her lamp fills up with gold, and it stays on. One long, slow, quiet glow.

Blink, blink, quick. Glow, glow, slow. Every little light says hello.

Fen tries again. She squeezes tight. She thinks quick, quick, quick thoughts.

But her lamp does not listen to her. It just grows warm and gold, and it stays, and stays, and stays.

A quick little firefly rushes right past her nose. Blink! Blink! And he is gone into the dark.

Fen looks down at her own light, and her wings go slow and heavy.

"You do it wrong," she says to her lamp. "You do it all wrong."

Fen flies down into the grass. She creeps under her big green leaf and makes herself very small.

Above the leaf the meadow blinks and blinks and blinks, quick and happy, without her.

Fen turns her lamp down to the cool ground so that she cannot see it any more.

She does not want to glow tonight. She does not want to glow ever again.

One round tear sits on her cheek, shiny like a little drop of dew.

Then someone lands softly on the leaf beside her. It is Mama. Her wings smell like grass and rain.

"My lamp is broken," says Fen. "It will not blink. It only glows and glows and glows."

Mama takes the little lamp gently in her hands and turns it over, this way and that way.

"Feel it," she says. "Is it hot, like the sun? Is it hot, like a candle?"

Fen touches her lamp. It is bright, and it is cool. Not hot at all, like a tiny piece of moon.

"Now watch me," says Mama. She lifts her own lamp up high above the leaf.

It fills up with gold. Slow. Long. Warm. Exactly, exactly like Fen's.

"Every kind of firefly has its own light," says Mama. "The quick ones blink quick, quick, quick. We glow slow."

"It is how we say our name out loud in the dark, so that our family can find us."

"Your lamp is not broken, little one. Your lamp is saying Fen."

Mama glows once, long and slow. Fen holds her breath and waits in the dark grass.

Far, far away, a light answers her. Slow and gold. Then another one. Then three more.

"Those are ours," says Mama. "They are your family, and they heard you. They are saying hello back."

Fen glows as long and as bright as she can, and all across the meadow the slow gold lights glow back at her.

Blink, blink, quick. Glow, glow, slow. Every little light says hello.

Late in the night the meadow grows quiet. One by one, the quick lights go out.

Cold dew sits on the grass, round and shiny like tiny glass beads, and the moon is low and soft.

Fen folds her wings and lies down close to Mama, under the big green leaf.

Her little lamp glows slow, and slower, until it is only a small warm ember in the dark.

Blink, blink, quick. Glow, glow, slow. Good night, little light. Good night.

Before we sleep

  1. What would you do if your light looked different from everybody else's light?
  2. If you could write your name in light, would it be quick and blinky, or long and slow?

Tomorrow you can take a torch into a dark room and make up your own light pattern: two long, three quick. Then let somebody else try to copy it, and see if they can say your name in light.

Good night

The grass is dark and quiet, every little lamp is low, and you are sleeping until morning.

For grown-ups: what is true here, and sources

Firefly flashes really are a language. Each kind (species) of firefly has its own flash pattern: how long each flash lasts, how many flashes there are, and how long the pause is between them. A male signals with his species' pattern, and a female of the same species answers with a flash of her own, which is how the two find each other in a meadow full of other fireflies. Some kinds give quick repeated blinks, some give one long slow glow, and in a single field several kinds can be signalling at once.

A firefly's light is a "cold light". Inside the lantern at the tip of the abdomen, a substance called luciferin reacts with oxygen, helped by an enzyme called luciferase, and almost all of the energy comes out as light instead of heat. That is why Fen's lamp is bright but cool to touch, and it is the one thing in the story you can promise a child is exactly true.

Two simplifications to know about: in real fireflies the flashing conversation is courtship between adult males and females, not a mother answering her child, and a firefly cannot simply decide to copy another species' pattern the way Fen tries to. The feeling is the story's; the flash patterns and the cold light are not.
